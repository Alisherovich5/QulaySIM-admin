{% trans "Where prices come from" %}

{% blocktrans trimmed %} Both wholesalers are read straight from their own APIs, and the catalogue is built from what they actually sell. Nobody types a destination in: the sync creates the countries the suppliers cover, gives each one its tariff ladder, and records every supplier's price for every tariff. {% endblocktrans %}

{% blocktrans trimmed %} Both suppliers' prices are kept, not just the winner. The cheapest available one becomes the tariff's cost and the route a paid order takes; the dearer one stays on file as the fallback for when the winner is out of stock or its API is down. Customers never see any of this — they see one tariff at one price. {% endblocktrans %}

{% blocktrans trimmed %} Supplier costs move retail prices through the pricing rules, so a sync can change what the site charges. That is the point, and it is also why a preview writes nothing and why newly discovered destinations arrive switched off — waiting for someone to look at them before they go on sale. {% endblocktrans %}
